# Performance Improvement Program
## Delivery Partner Benefit & Quality Framework

### 1. Executive Summary

This case study addresses two operational challenges for a hyperlocal delivery platform: improving delivery-partner availability and identifying partners requiring performance intervention.

The proposed solution combines a simple supply-hour incentive program with a transparent quality scorecard.

The benefit program uses four supply-hour slabs, day-based demand multipliers, and a weekly consistency bonus. At ₹0.50 per point, the model produces an overall CPT of **₹0.350**, with the highest monthly CPT at **₹0.374**, below the ₹0.90 ceiling.

The quality framework scores eight partner-performance metrics against defined thresholds and classifies partners into Gold, Silver, or Retraining buckets.

---

### 2. Business Objective

The business needs a delivery fleet that is:

- Available for longer durations
- Available consistently throughout the week
- Particularly available on high-demand days
- Cost-efficient
- Reliable from a customer-experience perspective

The program therefore needs to balance **partner motivation, supply coverage, quality, and cost**.

---

### 3. Part 1 – Benefit Program

#### 3.1 Design Principles

The target partner population earns approximately ₹20,000–₹30,000 per month. Therefore, the program should avoid complicated calculations.

The design principles are:

1. Easy to understand.
2. Reward additional availability.
3. Reward high-demand days.
4. Encourage weekday consistency.
5. Maintain CPT below ₹0.90.
6. Make the program easy to monitor.

#### 3.2 Point Structure

| Supply Hours | Base Points |
|---|---:|
| <4 | 0 |
| 4–<8 | 4 |
| 8–<12 | 8 |
| ≥12 | 12 |

#### 3.3 Demand Multiplier

| Day | Multiplier |
|---|---:|
| Sunday | 1.50 |
| Monday | 1.50 |
| Friday | 1.25 |
| Saturday | 1.25 |
| Tuesday | 1.00 |
| Wednesday | 1.00 |
| Thursday | 1.00 |

Formula:

**Daily Points = Base Points × Day Multiplier**

#### 3.4 Consistency Bonus

A partner who is active on at least five days during a calendar week receives an additional **10 points**.

This prevents partners from concentrating activity only on high-demand days.

#### 3.5 Point Value

**1 point = ₹0.50**

This value was selected so that the model provides a meaningful incentive while preserving sufficient CPT headroom.

### 6. Part 2 – Partner Quality Model

The quality model evaluates eight dimensions:

- Acceptance
- Cancellation
- Delivery completion
- Partner-driven cancellation
- Delivery time
- Support interaction
- Customer contact
- Customer rating

The weights reflect the business impact of reliability and customer experience while retaining productivity and efficiency measures.

#### Quality Score

| Metric | Weight |
|---|---:|
| Acceptance Rate | 15 |
| Cancellation Rate | 15 |
| Tasks Not Delivered | 15 |
| Partner Cancellation | 10 |
| Task Time | 15 |
| Interaction Rate | 10 |
| Contact Rate | 5 |
| Rating | 15 |
| **Total** | **100** |

A metric receives its assigned weight when the partner meets the target threshold. Otherwise, the metric receives zero for the first-pass screening score.

This approach is intentionally transparent and suitable for operational use.

---

### 7. Threshold Framework

| Metric | Threshold | Business Rationale |
|---|---|---|
| Acceptance Rate | ≥90% | Encourages reliable acceptance of assigned work |
| Cancellation Rate | ≤2% | Limits service disruption |
| Tasks Not Delivered | ≤1% | Protects fulfillment quality |
| Partner Cancellation | ≤1% | Controls partner-driven failures |
| Task Time | ≤45 min | Supports timely delivery |
| Interaction Rate | ≤40% | Flags excessive support dependency |
| Contact Rate | ≤15% | Limits avoidable customer contacts |
| Rating | ≥4.7 | Protects customer experience |

---

### 8. Partner Segmentation

#### Gold – 85 to 100

High-performing partners.

Suggested action:

- Recognition
- Priority incentive opportunities
- Retention initiatives

#### Silver – 70 to 84

Partners broadly meeting expectations but with improvement opportunities.

Suggested action:

- Coaching
- Periodic monitoring

#### Retraining – Below 70

Partners requiring structured intervention.

Suggested action:

- Root-cause analysis
- Targeted retraining
- Monitoring period

---

### 9. Retraining Process

#### First Retraining Event

- Identify failed metrics.
- Conduct root-cause analysis.
- Deliver targeted refresher training.
- Monitor performance for one week.

#### Second Event

- One-to-one coaching.
- Two-week improvement plan.
- More frequent performance monitoring.

#### Third Event

- Formal PIP.
- Manager review.
- Define measurable improvement targets.

#### Repeated Failure

Escalate for operational or contractual review according to company policy.

This progressive approach gives partners an opportunity to improve while protecting customer and business outcomes.

---

### 10. Risks and Mitigations

| Risk | Mitigation |
|---|---|
| Partners optimize only for hours | Add task-quality eligibility rules |
| Weekend concentration | Weekly consistency bonus |
| Incentive overspend | Monthly CPT monitoring |
| Gaming of supply hours | Validate online hours against operational activity |
| Unfair retraining decisions | Use transparent metric-level scoring |
| Temporary performance dip | Review multiple periods before severe action |

---

### 11. KPIs for Program Monitoring

The program should be monitored through:

- Average supply hours per partner
- Active partners per day
- High-demand-day supply coverage
- Completed tasks
- Incentive cost
- CPT
- Acceptance rate
- Cancellation rate
- Tasks not delivered
- Average task time
- Partner rating
- Retraining rate

---

### 12. Recommended Governance

**Weekly:** monitor supply, quality and retraining candidates.

**Monthly:** review CPT, incentive ROI and partner behavior.

**Quarterly:** recalibrate point values, thresholds and business-day multipliers based on observed outcomes.

This makes the program adaptive rather than a one-time intervention.

---

### 13. Conclusion

The proposed Performance Improvement Program creates a direct connection between partner availability and business demand while keeping incentive costs controlled.

The benefit program is deliberately simple: four supply-hour slabs, three multiplier levels, one consistency rule and a fixed point value.

The quality framework complements the incentive program by ensuring that partners are not rewarded solely for availability. Service quality, reliability, efficiency and customer experience remain critical.

The combination creates a balanced framework for **Supply + Cost + Quality + Customer Experience**.

## Interview Talking Point

> “My approach was to avoid designing an overly complicated incentive system. I first translated the business requirement into a simple supply-hour model, then added demand-based multipliers because Sunday and Monday have higher supply requirements. I also added a consistency bonus so partners would not optimize only for peak days. Finally, I validated the financial feasibility using CPT and designed a separate quality scorecard so that availability does not come at the expense of service quality.”
